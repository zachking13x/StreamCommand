Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 22, 2026

The short version

Stream Command runs on your PC and keeps your data there. It does not collect analytics or usage data, there is no Stream Command account, and the developer does not run any server that your data passes through.

What is stored on your PC

Stream Command saves the following in %LocalAppData%\StreamCommand on your computer:

  • Your Twitch sign-in token and username, used to show your chat, send the messages you type, and read your own channel's follower, viewer and event information.
  • Your OBS WebSocket port and password, used to control OBS Studio on the same PC.
  • Any optional keys you choose to enter in Settings for other streaming services.
  • Your checklists, stream planner entries, automation rules and app preferences.

Sign-in tokens, passwords and keys are encrypted with Windows Data Protection, so they can only be read by your own Windows user account on that PC.

If you use the Clips feature, stream recordings and clips are saved only to your own Videos folder (or a folder you choose), and are never uploaded anywhere.

Who Stream Command talks to

  • Twitch, directly from your PC, for sign-in, chat and your channel's own stats and events.
  • OBS Studio on your own PC, over a local connection that never leaves your computer. Connections to other computers are refused.

Links in the app, such as Quick Launch and Streamer Tools, simply open those websites in your browser. Stream Command does not send your information to them.

What is never collected

No analytics, crash reports, advertising identifiers, location or contact details are sent anywhere. The developer never receives your Twitch account details, your OBS password, your chat or your recordings.

Microsoft Store

Stream Command is installed and licensed through the Microsoft Store. Microsoft handles everything about your Store account and payment, and Stream Command never sees your payment details. Microsoft's own privacy statement applies to the Store.

Removing your data

To remove everything Stream Command has stored, uninstall the app and delete the %LocalAppData%\StreamCommand folder. You can also revoke Stream Command's access at any time from your Twitch account's Connections page.

Children

Stream Command does not collect personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the updated version will be published on this page with a new date.
